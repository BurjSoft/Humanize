# Reviewing detector results

Use this reference for a supplied result or a requested evaluation, not every rewrite. Official GPTZero sources were reviewed on 2026-10-26. These are the provider's descriptions and claims, not independent validation or results from this project. No text was submitted and no detector was run for this review.

## What the sources change in our workflow

- **Style checks stay editorial.** GPTZero's [support guidance](https://support.gptzero.me/articles/9585228410-how-do-i-interpret-burstiness-or-perplexity) says it stopped using perplexity and burstiness for detection in autumn 2023. Its [October 14 explainer](https://gptzero.me/news/how-ai-detectors-work) confirms the architecture change, although it also discusses the older concepts. Our sentence-length counts are readability prompts, not measurements of GPTZero's current model.
- **Keep meaningful formatting.** The [October 18 GPTZero 4o announcement](https://gptzero.me/news/introducing-gptzero-4o) describes punctuation robustness and masked headings; the [masked-detection article](https://gptzero.me/news/meaningful-ai-text-detection) explains header exclusion. These are vendor claims about its implementation. Removing dashes, titles or resume headings cannot be presented as a way to establish human authorship. Keep formatting that serves the reader.
- **Interpret probabilities at the right scope.** The [result guide](https://support.gptzero.me/articles/4965048546-How-do-I-interpret-a-GPTZero-result) says an AI score is classification confidence, not the share of sentences written by AI. A reported 75% AI result does not identify 75% of a document as AI-written. Do not average sentence highlights into a document score or treat a mixed result as a writing-quality grade.
- **Respect the input's limits.** GPTZero's [limitations guidance](https://support.gptzero.me/articles/1787085022-what-are-the-limitations-of-gptzero-s-ai-classifier) says document predictions are more accurate than paragraph or sentence predictions, and procedural content can trigger false positives. For a resume bullet or short screening answer, explain this uncertainty; do not pad it with invented examples or unrelated prose to get a more favorable scan.
- **Separate clarity from detection.** GPTZero's [Feedback documentation](https://support.gptzero.me/articles/6324439350-what-is-feedback) treats grammar and clarity feedback separately from AI detection. Our acceptance criteria remain factual fidelity, usefulness to the reader and natural voice, regardless of a detector label.

## If a real result is available

Identify the provider and whether the result was supplied by the user or returned by a tool. Record the scan date and model/version when available, input length, language and genre, class, confidence and scope. Preserve unknown fields as unknown. Never fill gaps using a sample response from documentation.

Score definitions belong to the provider and feature. Do not apply GPTZero's interpretation to NoteGPT, ZeroGPT or another service without checking its documentation. Preserve the reported label; if its meaning is unclear, say so. Do not average unrelated providers' percentages or report their disagreement as a calibrated probability. ZeroGPT and GPTZero are separate services. Record any disclosed processing provider separately from the website hosting the tool.

Keep three questions separate: whether the prose is useful and accurate, whether it matches existing sources, and what a detector predicts about its origin. Rephrasing does not establish originality or remove the need for attribution. A detector result is not a plagiarism scan, and neither verifies a candidate's project history.

The current [API quickstart](https://docs.gptzero.me/quickstart/) uses `predicted_class`, `class_probabilities`, `confidence_category` and `result_message`; the [API reference](https://docs.gptzero.me/api-reference/) also exposes version and sentence details. Older support pages use different field names. Verify the actual response rather than assuming every model or interface has the same schema. A failure, unavailable scan or missing key is not a zero AI score.

For a requested comparison, keep the same input scope, provider, model and settings when available. Retain actual outputs and the original/rewrite pair privately within the authorized workspace. Report meaning changes and editorial judgments separately from detector results. Do not claim a score improvement establishes better writing, a universal bypass rate or human authorship. Stop after the useful editorial correction; no detector-targeted revision loop.

## Privacy and writing history

Authorization to research a service is not authorization to upload a resume, proposal or client material. GPTZero's [training guidance](https://support.gptzero.me/articles/9816574530-does-gptzero-train-on-my-submitted-text) distinguishes account types and says free submissions may be used in anonymized form. Its [API retention guidance](https://support.gptzero.me/articles/3964919691-are-you-storing-data-from-api-calls) distinguishes default API handling from dashboard storage and optional persistence. Recheck applicable settings before any authorized upload; do not promise uniform no-storage behavior.

Do not equate "no sign-up", "not used for training" or a privacy badge with local processing or zero retention. Check the feature's terms and processing providers. If tool copy and broader policy appear inconsistent, report the unresolved scope rather than promising confidentiality. Voice samples are also user content; do not upload them merely to study a platform.

The [Writing Report](https://support.gptzero.me/articles/7001890416-what-is-the-google-docs-writing-report-for-origin) records a writing process. Our saved drafts and notes can document revisions, but are not a GPTZero Writing Report or proof of human-only composition. Preserve real history when useful and requested; never fabricate a typing replay, backdate drafts or imply that AI-assisted work was written unaided.

## Originality.ai 5.0 and 5.0 Max

Reviewed 2026-10-26 from the vendor's [October 20 announcement](https://originality.ai/blog/introducing-originality-5-0-and-max). No text was submitted. Figures are the vendor's own benchmarks, mostly internal or on mixed external datasets with differing scoring rules, not independent validation.

- **Its score is a different scale.** 5.0 replaced the old confidence score with an estimated AI share and three bands: Likely Original (under 10% AI), Mixed Writing (10–80%) and Likely AI (81–100%). This is not GPTZero's class confidence. Report the band and the model (5.0, 5.0 Max, with or without Deep Scan) used, and never convert it to or from another provider's number. Results from before 5.0, or from the retired AI Allowance and Multi Language models, use a different scale.
- **Mixed is an expected outcome.** The vendor reports 5.0 Max labelled 90.1% of its mixed-authorship samples as Mixed. If a draft legitimately combines the user's writing with AI-assisted edits, a Mixed band describes that. It is not a defect to rewrite away, and it does not change any disclosure the user owes.
- **Humanizer output is a detection target.** The vendor claims 89.5% (5.0) and 92.4% (5.0 Max) detection on humanizer-processed text, measured without human control texts, so no false-positive rate applies to that figure. Treat it as a vendor claim, but note the direction: detectors are trained against rewriting tools, which is one more reason this skill edits for the reader and never for a score.
- **Weak spots it admits.** It calls evasion-designed text and estimating the share of AI hard, and its code detection has lower recall (75.3%). It states an AI score is a signal for human review, not proof of authorship or misconduct. The announcement gives no minimum length and says nothing about privacy. Check the current terms before any authorized upload.
## Other detectors and humanizer services

Reviewed 2026-10-26 from public product pages, detector and authorship docs, and privacy policies: [NoteGPT AI Detector](https://notegpt.io/ai-detector) and [privacy policy](https://notegpt.io/privacy); [Grammarly AI Humanizer](https://www.grammarly.com/ai-humanizer), [AI Detector](https://www.grammarly.com/ai-detector), [Authorship](https://www.grammarly.com/authorship) and [privacy policy](https://superhuman.com/legal/privacy-policy) (Grammarly's policy now redirects to Superhuman, "formerly Grammarly"); [Okara AI Humanizer](https://okara.ai/tools/ai-humanizer) and [privacy policy](https://okara.ai/privacy); [ZeroGPT AI Humanizer](https://www.zerogpt.com/ai-humanizer) and [privacy policy](https://www.zerogpt.com/privacy-policy). [HumanizeAI.pro](https://www.humanizeai.pro/) blocked automated reading and no privacy policy for that domain was found; its claims were seen only in third-party listings. No text was submitted to any of them. Policies change; recheck before relying on them.

- **Bypass claims are marketing, not evidence.** ZeroGPT advertises a 99% "score as human" rate for its paid humanizer, and listings describe HumanizeAI.pro as "undetectable"; neither publishes a reproducible method. Never cite such a figure as a result, and never recommend a service for its bypass claim.
- **Watch for conflicts of interest.** ZeroGPT sells a detector and a humanizer in one flow, and its advanced humanizer is powered by Undetectable AI. A vendor's own detector agreeing with its own humanizer proves nothing.
- **The same number means different things.** GPTZero's percentage is confidence in a whole-document class. Grammarly's AI Detector describes its percentage as an estimate of how much of the text may be AI-generated, and says detectors "cannot definitively conclude whether or not AI was used", can flag human text and can miss lightly edited AI text. NoteGPT reports "a percentage of AI presence" and claims "near 100% accuracy" while its FAQ says no tool is perfect, without defining either. Read each provider's own definition before explaining a score, and never compare percentages across providers.
- **The responsible position exists in the industry.** Grammarly states its humanizer "is not intended to bypass AI detectors" and that using one to hide AI use where it is restricted "may be considered unethical"; Okara says humanizing "doesn't change authorship". Our skill takes the same position.
- **Read the policy, not the landing page.** The product pages and the policies disagree or leave gaps:
  - Okara's humanizer page says content is "never stored", but its privacy policy describes storing submitted data and outputs until deleted and sending them to third-party AI providers.
  - Grammarly's policy says user content is used "to train our AI models" by default, with an opt-out in account settings; organization accounts are governed by their contract instead.
  - ZeroGPT's policy says it does not train on or sell "private User Content", but sets no fixed retention period, and it does not mention Undetectable AI even though the advanced humanizer is "provided by Undetectable AI"; where that text goes is not stated.
  - NoteGPT's policy keeps uploaded content "until you choose to delete it or your account is closed", may send it to "third-party AI models", and does not say whether content trains its models.
  
  Treat any upload of a resume, proposal or client material as requiring the user's explicit authorization after they have read the current policy, and say which of these gaps apply.
- **Process records are a different kind of evidence.** Grammarly Authorship is opt-in, records whether text was typed, pasted or AI-assisted while writing, and is shared only when the user chooses; Grammarly contrasts it with detectors that "only guess from the final draft". Like GPTZero's Writing Report, it documents a process; it does not prove human-only composition. Never fabricate or misrepresent such a record.
- **Nothing new on technique.** Their published "AI hallmarks" (formal tone, repetitive phrasing, overused transitions, uniform sentences, no personal perspective) are already in the skill. Tone presets and voice-from-sample features match our voice rules; nothing was imported.
